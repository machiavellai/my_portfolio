How a page reaches the reader

Kingdom Comics delivers 40-page issues to phones that are usually on hotel wifi or a train, so the reader must never wait on the network for a page the device already holds. Every page is encoded once into three WebP widths, written to a content-addressed bucket, and described by a per-issue manifest the app stores on first open. The manifest is the only mutable thing in the system; the pages are immutable, which is what makes a stale cache a correct cache rather than a bug to invalidate.

Prefetch is where it broke. Reading two pages ahead is the obvious optimisation, and it is also the thing that starves the page the reader is looking at — a background issue download and a speculative prefetch will take every connection the phone will give them. The fix was one queue with two priorities and a rule with no exceptions: a user-visible fetch cancels every speculative fetch in flight before it starts.

reader/prefetch.ts Copy
// a page the reader can see outranks anything speculative const job = queue.push({ url, priority: visible ? 'now' : 'idle' }); if (visible) queue.cancel('idle'); // no exceptions

That is the whole architecture worth writing down: immutable pages, one manifest, one queue. Median page turn on a cold cache measured 180ms against a throttled 3G profile, and no reader has seen a half-rendered spread since the queue landed.

It's that past three items the comma list stops reading as a sentence — the separators between claims are indistinguishable from the separators inside them. The fix isn't a copy edit, it's a render rule in the component: ≤2 items → inline sentence (lead-in + em-dash + comma-joined). ≥3 items → stacked (lead-in as its own sentence, each item a line). One CMS array, two renderings, chosen by evidence.length — you add a fourth domain and the layout adapts without a deploy.

Two constraints this puts on the field: evidenceLead must end without punctuation (the component appends the em-dash or the period), and items must be ≤55 chars to stay on one line each at desktop (Inter 14.5px on a 448px measure fits ~58 — 55 leaves slack for a wider glyph run). Both belong in the CMS field description.
